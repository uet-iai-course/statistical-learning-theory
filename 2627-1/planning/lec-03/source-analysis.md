## 2. Theorem 3.5: a symmetrization proof with an explicit constant

**Setting.** $S=(Z_1..Z_m)$, ghost sample $S'=(Z'_1..Z'_m)$, all $2m$ i.i.d. $D$; 0–1 loss; $\mathcal H$ fixed before sampling; measurability (M) of §3. $\epsilon>0$.

**Step 1 (ghost-sample lemma).** If $m\epsilon^2\ge2$: $\Pr[\sup_h\lvert L_D(h)-L_S(h)\rvert>\epsilon]\le2\Pr[\sup_h\lvert L_{S'}(h)-L_S(h)\rvert>\epsilon/2]$.
Proof: on $A=\{\sup\lvert L_D-L_S\rvert>\epsilon\}$ pick (measurably, first index in the countable $\mathcal H_0$ of (M)) $h_S$ with $\lvert L_D(h_S)-L_S(h_S)\rvert>\epsilon$. Given $S$, $h_S$ is fixed and $S'$ is independent of $S$, so Chebyshev (Bổ đề 02.3) with $\mathrm{Var}\,\ell\le\frac14$: $\Pr[\lvert L_{S'}(h_S)-L_D(h_S)\rvert\ge\frac\epsilon2\mid S]\le\frac{1/4}{m\epsilon^2/4}=\frac1{m\epsilon^2}\le\frac12$. On the complement, $\lvert L_{S'}(h_S)-L_S(h_S)\rvert\ge\lvert L_D-L_S\rvert-\lvert L_{S'}-L_D\rvert>\epsilon-\frac\epsilon2=\frac\epsilon2$. Hence $\Pr[\sup\lvert L_{S'}-L_S\rvert>\frac\epsilon2]\ge\mathbb E[\mathbb 1_A\cdot\frac12]=\frac12\Pr(A)$. (Hoeffding instead of Chebyshev would need $2e^{-m\epsilon^2/2}\le\frac12$, i.e. $m\epsilon^2\ge2\ln4=2.77$; Chebyshev is better here.) Independence of $S'$ from $S$ and boundedness are used here.

**Step 2 (swap / random signs).** Swapping $Z_i\leftrightarrow Z'_i$ for any set of indices leaves the law of $(S,S')$ unchanged (the pairs are i.i.d. and each pair is exchangeable). So for $\sigma_i$ i.i.d. uniform on $\{\pm1\}$ independent of $(S,S')$, the process $(L_{S'}(h)-L_S(h))_h$ has the same law as $(\frac1m\sum_i\sigma_ia_i(h))_h$ with $a_i(h)=\ell(h,Z'_i)-\ell(h,Z_i)\in\{-1,0,1\}$.

**Step 3 (reduce to label patterns on the combined sample).** Condition on $(S,S')$. The vector $(a_i(h))_i$ depends on $h$ only through $h$'s restriction to the $2m$-tuple $(X_1..X_m,X'_1..X'_m)$: at most $\Pi_{\mathcal H}(2m)$ distinct vectors (tuple definition). **This is why the ghost sample is needed:** $L_D(h)$ is *not* a function of $h$'s pattern on $S$ (two hypotheses with the same pattern on $S$ can have different $L_D$), and the pattern set on $S$ is random — a union bound over it is a union over a data-chosen class, which Lecture 02's (T4) forbids. After symmetrization every quantity depends on $h$ only through finitely many patterns, and the randomness left ($\sigma$) is independent of those patterns.

**Step 4 (concentration + union bound).** For a fixed pattern, the $\sigma_ia_i$ are independent, mean 0, in $[-1,1]$. Hoeffding (Định lý 02.7 with Nhận xét 02.8, ranges of length 2): $\Pr_\sigma[\lvert\frac1m\sum\sigma_ia_i\rvert\ge t]\le2\exp(-2m^2t^2/(4m))=2e^{-mt^2/2}$; at $t=\epsilon/2$: $2e^{-m\epsilon^2/8}$. (Equivalently apply the $[0,1]$ version to $(\sigma_ia_i+1)/2$ at deviation $t/2$: $2e^{-2m(t/2)^2}$, same.) Union bound over $\le\Pi_{\mathcal H}(2m)$ patterns, then average over $(S,S')$: $\Pr[\sup\lvert L_{S'}-L_S\rvert>\frac\epsilon2]\le2\Pi_{\mathcal H}(2m)e^{-m\epsilon^2/8}$ ("$>$" is contained in "$\ge$").

**Step 5 (combine, invert).** For $m\epsilon^2\ge2$: $\Pr[\sup\lvert L_D-L_S\rvert>\epsilon]\le4\,\Pi_{\mathcal H}(2m)\,e^{-m\epsilon^2/8}$. Setting the right side to $\delta$: $\epsilon_m=\sqrt{8\ln(4\Pi_{\mathcal H}(2m)/\delta)/m}$. **The condition $m\epsilon^2\ge2$ is automatic:** $m\epsilon_m^2=8\ln(4\Pi/\delta)\ge8\ln4=11.09>2$ since $\Pi\ge1$, $\delta<1$. No clipping needed for it; clipping at 1 is still allowed because $\sup\lvert L_D-L_S\rvert\le1$ always.

**Step 6 (Sauer with $2m$).** If $1\le d\le m$ then $d\le2m$ and $\Pi(2m)\le(2em/d)^d$, so $\epsilon_m\le\sqrt{8(d\ln(2em/d)+\ln(4/\delta))/m}$.

**Blueprint form, explicit $C$.** $d\ln(2em/d)=d\ln2+d\ln(em/d)\le(1+\ln2)\,d\ln(em/d)$ because $\ln(em/d)\ge1$ when $m\ge d$ (checked numerically for $d<60$, $m<400$); $\ln(4/\delta)=\ln2+\ln(2/\delta)\le2\ln(2/\delta)$ because $\ln(2/\delta)>\ln2$. So $8(\dots)\le8\max(1.693,2)(d\ln(em/d)+\ln(2/\delta))=16(\dots)$: **$C=4$.** Numerically the $C=4$ form is 1.33–1.37 times the direct form (table §4). The $\ln(2em/d)$ vs $\ln(em/d)$ difference is absorbed in the $1+\ln2$ factor.

**Recommended statement (course):** under (T1), 0–1 loss, $\mathcal H$ fixed before sampling with (M), for every $m\ge1$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$, with probability at least $1-\delta$ over $S\sim D^m$,
$\sup_h\lvert L_D(h)-L_S(h)\rvert\le\sqrt{8\ln(4\Pi_{\mathcal H}(2m)/\delta)/m}$; if moreover $1\le d\le m$, this is $\le\sqrt{8(d\ln(2em/d)+\ln(4/\delta))/m}\le4\sqrt{(d\ln(em/d)+\ln(2/\delta))/m}$. All right sides may be replaced by $\min\{1,\cdot\}$. Proof [S] in body (Steps 1–6 as a roadmap), details in a Phụ lục.

**Why not UML 6.11.** Its $1/\delta$ comes from Markov on $\mathbb E\sup$; at $d=3$, $m=10^4$, $\delta=0.05$: printed form $(4+\sqrt{29.415})/(0.05\sqrt{20000})=1.333$ (vacuous), corrected form $2.100$, ours $0.164$. At $m=10^6$: $0.150$ / $0.243$ / $0.0195$. Shrinking $\delta$ ten-fold multiplies UML's bound by 10, ours by about $\sqrt{1+\ln10/\ln(4\Pi/\delta)}$. The blueprint also explicitly asks for $\ln(2/\delta)$.

**Comparison with Lecture 02.** For the six screening thresholds ($\Pi(2m)\le6$), $m=1000$, $\delta=0.05$: VC route $\sqrt{8\ln480/1000}=\sqrt{8\cdot6.1738/1000}=0.2222$; finite-class $r_m=\sqrt{\ln240/2000}=\sqrt{5.4806/2000}=0.0523$. Symmetrization costs a factor $\approx4.25$ (variance proxy 8 vs $\frac12$: deviation halved and range doubled). The note must not suggest the VC bound is sharper for finite classes.

## 3. Measurability for infinite classes

Standard qualification (Pollard's "permissible" classes; a simple sufficient version for the course): **(M)** there is a countable $\mathcal H_0\subseteq\mathcal H$ such that every $h\in\mathcal H$ is the pointwise limit of a sequence in $\mathcal H_0$. Then for every sample, $\sup_{\mathcal H}\lvert L_S-L_D\rvert=\sup_{\mathcal H_0}\lvert L_S-L_D\rvert$ ($L_S$ is eventually equal along the sequence since only finitely many points matter; $L_D(h_k)\to L_D(h)$ by dominated convergence), same for $\sup\lvert L_{S'}-L_S\rvert$, so all events in §2 are countable unions/intersections, and the selection $h_S$ in Step 1 can be the first index in $\mathcal H_0$. Checks: thresholds $\mathbb 1[x\ge t]$: rational $t_k\uparrow t$ (not $\downarrow$, which converges to $\mathbb 1[x>t]$). Closed intervals: rational $a_k\uparrow a$, $b_k\downarrow b$. Affine halfspaces $\mathbb 1[\langle w,x\rangle+b\ge0]$: rational $w_k$ with $\lVert w_k-w\rVert\le1/k^2$, rational $b_k\in[b+1/k,b+2/k]$; at a boundary point $x$, $\langle w_k,x\rangle+b_k\ge-\lVert x\rVert/k^2+1/k\ge0$ once $k\ge\lVert x\rVert$; off the boundary the sign is eventually correct. So all classes of the lecture satisfy (M). This discharges Lecture 01 Phụ lục A(v) for Lecture 03. Mention that without such a condition the fundamental theorem can fail for pathological classes (statement only, [O]; do not invent an example). ERM algorithms on infinite classes additionally need Def. 01.4 joint measurability, assumed, not proved.

**Parameter count vs VC dimension.** (a) One parameter, $d=\infty$: $h_\theta(x)=\mathbb 1[\sin(\theta x)>0]$ (UML §6.3.5 and Ex. 6.8 write $\lceil\sin(\theta x)\rceil$ with $\lceil-1\rceil=0$; same function). Construction verified by exact rational arithmetic for all $2^k$ labelings, $k\le10$: points $x_i=2^{-i}$, $i=1..k$, $\theta=\pi\big(1+\sum_{j=1}^k(1-y_j)2^j\big)$. Proof: $\theta x_i/\pi=\sum_{j\ge i}(1-y_j)2^{j-i}+\big(1+\sum_{j<i}(1-y_j)2^j\big)2^{-i}$; integer part has parity $1-y_i$; fractional part lies in $(0,1)$ because $1\le1+\sum_{j<i}(\cdot)2^j\le2^i-1$; $\sin(\pi(N+f))=(-1)^N\sin(\pi f)$ with $\sin(\pi f)>0$, so $h_\theta(x_i)=y_i$. UML's hint variant (points $2^m$, $\theta=\pi x$ with binary digits $1-y_m$ and a trailing 1) also checks out. (b) Many parameters, small $d$: $h_w(x)=\mathbb 1[(w_1+\dots+w_p)x\ge0]$ on $\mathbb R$ realises only 3 functions ($\mathbb 1[x\ge0]$, $\mathbb 1[x\le0]$, $\equiv1$): $d=1$ for every $p$ (one point $x=1$ shattered; $\{-1,1\}$ misses $(0,0)$; any pair $u<v$ misses a pattern). Also finite classes: $d\le\log_2\lvert\mathcal H\rvert$ regardless of parameters.

## 5. "Remove an assumption" counterexamples

- **(a) Infinite VC.** $\mathcal M_{[0,1]}$, $D$: $X\sim U[0,1]$, $Y=1$ (Lecture 01 Ví dụ 01.8, Mệnh đề 01.15): the memorizer $\mathbb 1_F\in\mathcal H$ has $L_S=0$, $L_D=1$ on every sample, so $\sup_h\lvert L_D-L_S\rvert=1$ surely: uniform convergence fails for every $m$, and by Cor 6.4 *no* algorithm PAC-learns the class. Keep Mệnh đề 01.15(c) in view: infinite VC does not make every ERM fail on every $D$ (constant-1 ERM succeeds there); the failure is distribution-free. Recommended (closes the Lecture 01 promise).
- **(b) Class chosen after the sample (keeps $d$ small!).** $\mathcal H_S=\{h_{\equiv0},\mathbb 1_F\}$ with $F=\{X_1..X_m\}$: two functions, VC dimension 1, but chosen from $S$. Same $D$: $L_S(\mathbb 1_F)=0$, $L_D(\mathbb 1_F)=1$, deviation 1 with probability 1, while Theorem 3.5 with $d=1$, $m=1000$, $\delta=0.05$ would promise $\le0.322$ (direct) / $0.431$ ($C{=}4$). Shows "fixed before sampling" is essential, independent of $d$. Recommended.
- **(c) Non-i.i.d. (duplicate sample).** $S=(Z,\dots,Z)$, $Z\sim D_\eta$, six screening thresholds ($d=1$): if $Y=1$ then $L_S(h_1)=0$, $L_{D_\eta}(h_1)=0.52$; if $Y=0$ then $L_S(h_6)=0$, $L_{D_\eta}(h_6)=0.48$. So $\sup\ge0.48$ for every $m$, surely, exceeding 0.431 / 0.322 at $m=1000$ and 0.149 / 0.111 at $m=10^4$. Reuses Lecture 02 counterexample (a).
- **(d) Witness vs proof (blueprint §3.4, exit question).** Two symmetric errors: one shattered set of size $k$ proves only $d\ge k$ (e.g. 3 points for planar halfspaces); one *non*-shattered set of size $k$ proves nothing (three collinear points are not shattered by halfspaces, yet $d=3$; the square is not shattered by axis-aligned rectangles, yet $d=4$, UML Fig. 6.1).

## 6. Lower-bound promise (Lecture 02 Nhận xét 02.18, Lecture 01 Nhận xét 01.21)

UML Thm 6.8 (p. 72–73) states the bounds with "absolute constants $C_1,C_2$" and no range restrictions; that is too strong as written: UML's own lower-bound proofs need $\epsilon<1/(8\sqrt2)$ and $\delta<1/8$, and the realizable $\ln(1/\delta)/\epsilon$ term is false for $\mathcal X=\{c\}$, $\mathcal H=\{0,1\}^{\{c\}}$ ($d=1$): one example determines the label, so $m_{\mathcal H}(\epsilon,\delta)=1$ for all $\epsilon,\delta$. What UML proves (§28.2): (i) for $\epsilon<1/(8\sqrt2)$: if $m<d/(512\epsilon^2)$ then for every $A$ some $D$ has $\Pr[\text{excess}>\epsilon]\ge1/8$ (from $\mathbb E\Delta\ge1/4$, $\Delta\in[0,1]$, Lemma B.1, $\rho=8\epsilon$; re-derived: $d/(8\rho^2)=d/(512\epsilon^2)$; note the section heading claims "$8d/\epsilon^2$", inconsistent with its own derivation); (ii) for $\epsilon^2\le1/2$: $m\le\frac12\ln(1/(4\delta))/\epsilon^2$ forces failure probability $\ge\delta$ — **but its failure event is "excess $=\epsilon$"**, which does not violate "excess $\le\epsilon$"; running the construction at $2\epsilon$ fixes it ($m\le\ln(1/(4\delta))/(8\epsilon^2)$, $\epsilon<1/(2\sqrt2)$). I did not re-derive UML Lemma B.11. Realizable lower bound: stated only.

**Recommended course formulation (statement only, [O]-level citation to UML Thm 6.8 and §28.2):** *There are universal constants $c>0$ and $\epsilon_0,\delta_0\in(0,1)$ such that for every class $\mathcal H$ of binary classifiers with VC dimension $d$, $\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0)$, $\delta\in(0,\delta_0)$, every learning algorithm $A$ and every sample size $m$:* (a) *if $1\le d<\infty$ and $m<c\,(d+\ln(1/\delta))/\epsilon^2$, there is a distribution $D$ on $\mathcal X\times\{0,1\}$ with $\Pr_{S\sim D^m}[L_D(A(S))>\inf_{\mathcal H}L_D+\epsilon]>\delta$;* (b) *if $2\le d<\infty$ and $m<c\,(d+\ln(1/\delta))/\epsilon$, there is $D$ realizable by $\mathcal H$ with $\Pr[L_D(A(S))>\epsilon]>\delta$.* Equivalently $m_{\mathcal H}(\epsilon,\delta)\ge c(d+\ln(1/\delta))/\epsilon^2$ resp. $/\epsilon$, with $m_{\mathcal H}$ the minimum over algorithms (Định nghĩa 01.20, agnostic extension after Hệ quả 02.16). Add one sentence: the course's own upper bound (Cor 3.6 + Thm 3.5) gives $O((d\ln(d/\epsilon)+\ln(1/\delta))/\epsilon^2)$, a $\ln(1/\epsilon)$ factor above (a); removing it needs chaining (UML §28.1 remark). From UML one can quote explicit constants for (a) only in the split form $\max\{d/(512\epsilon^2),\ \ln(1/(4\delta))/(8\epsilon^2)\}$ (the latter with my fix), for $\epsilon<1/(8\sqrt2)$, $\delta<1/8$; I recommend keeping $c$ symbolic.

## 8. Delicate points for the writer

1. **$d$ vs $p$:** halfspaces in $\mathbb R^p$ have $d=p+1$ (affine), $d=p$ (homogeneous). UML uses $d$ for both feature dimension and (in Ch. 6) VC dimension; never in the same formula here.
2. **Witness vs equality:** one shattered set gives $d\ge k$; the upper bound needs *every* set of size $d+1$; a single non-shattered set proves nothing (collinear points).
3. **Growth function is a maximum over point tuples**, not over the given sample; $\Pi(m)=2^m$ for $m\le d$, $<2^m$ for $m>d$; use tuples so $\Pi$ is defined and nondecreasing for every $m$.
4. **Sauer's closed form needs $1\le d\le m$** (false at $m=1$, $d=3$); for $m<d$ the sum is just $2^m$. UML's "$m>d+1$" is a restriction of its proof, not of the result.
5. **Theorem 3.5 constants:** the symmetrization route gives $\sqrt{8\ln(4\Pi(2m)/\delta)/m}$ and $C=4$; the factor 8 comes from deviation $\epsilon/2$ and range 2 of $\sigma_ia_i$; $m\epsilon^2\ge2$ is automatic. Do not copy UML Thm 6.11 (wrong Hoeffding range, $1/\delta$).
6. **Why symmetrization:** $L_D(h)$ is not determined by $h$'s pattern on $S$, and patterns on $S$ form a data-chosen class; say this explicitly where the blueprint warns against the naive union bound.
7. **"With probability at least $1-\delta$"**: the failure event is "$>$", the guarantee "$\le$"; the probability is over $S$ (and $S'$, $\sigma$ only inside the proof).
8. **Class-level vs algorithm-level:** Thm 3.5 is a property of $\mathcal H$ and $D$-free; Cor 3.6 holds for *every* ERM on one event; the fundamental theorem's "PAC learnable" is existential. Lower bounds quantify over *all* algorithms (Lecture 01 Nhận xét 01.21).
9. **$\inf$ not $\min$** in Cor 3.6 and in the agnostic statements; ERM exists for 0–1 loss (finitely many values of $L_S$).
10. **Measurability:** state (M) once, verify it for thresholds ($t_k\uparrow t$), intervals, halfspaces; UML is silent.
11. **Threshold convention** $\mathbb 1[x\ge t]$ (course) vs UML $\mathbb 1[x<a]$; halfspace convention $\mathbb 1[\cdot\ge0]$ and the corresponding care in the dependence argument (§1, Prop. 3.3 row).
12. **Leave for Lecture 04:** no approximation–estimation decomposition, no SRM, no penalty selection, no "choose $d$ to balance"; at most the bridge sentence (richer class: smaller $\inf_{\mathcal H}L_D$, larger deviation bound). Rademacher complexity and the §28.1 route belong to Lecture 07.
13. **Multiclass and real-valued losses** are outside the theorem (blueprint §3.4 limit; UML Remark 6.3 notes learnability without uniform convergence in other settings).
14. **Numbers are conservative:** the VC sample sizes (e.g. 132 992 for $d=3$, $\epsilon=0.1$) exceed discretised finite-class sizes; present them as valid guarantees, not as estimates of what is needed.
