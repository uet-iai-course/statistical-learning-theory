## 2. Proofs and constants (all re-derived)

**2.1 Projection.** $W\ne\varnothing$ closed convex in $\mathbb R^p$. Existence: minimise $\lVert x-v\rVert$ over the compact $W\cap\bar B(v,\lVert w_0-v\rVert)$; uniqueness: strict convexity of $\lVert\cdot-v\rVert^2$ (parallelogram law). Characterisation: $\pi=\Pi_W(v)$ iff $\pi\in W$ and $\langle v-\pi,u-\pi\rangle\le0$ for all $u\in W$ (⇒ by differentiating $\alpha\mapsto\lVert\pi+\alpha(u-\pi)-v\rVert^2$ at $0^+$, as UML; ⇐ by expanding $\lVert u-v\rVert^2=\lVert u-\pi\rVert^2+\lVert\pi-v\rVert^2-2\langle v-\pi,u-\pi\rangle\ge\lVert\pi-v\rVert^2$). Pythagoras-type inequality: $\lVert v-u\rVert^2\ge\lVert\Pi_W v-u\rVert^2+\lVert v-\Pi_Wv\rVert^2$, hence Lemma 5.1. Nonexpansiveness: add the variational inequalities for $v,v'$ to get $\lVert\pi-\pi'\rVert^2\le\langle v-v',\pi-\pi'\rangle$, then Cauchy–Schwarz. Lemma 5.1 is the special case $v'=u\in W$. Recommendation: state Lemma 5.1 as in the blueprint plus the variational characterisation; nonexpansiveness as a remark.

**2.2 One step (pathwise, any $u\in W$).** $\lVert w_{t+1}-u\rVert^2\le\lVert w_t-\eta g_t-u\rVert^2$ (**the only place the projection and $u\in W$ enter**) $=\lVert w_t-u\rVert^2-2\eta\langle g_t,w_t-u\rangle+\eta^2\lVert g_t\rVert^2$, so $\langle g_t,w_t-u\rangle\le(\lVert w_t-u\rVert^2-\lVert w_{t+1}-u\rVert^2)/(2\eta)+\frac\eta2\lVert g_t\rVert^2$. No convexity, no probability used. Without projection it is an equality.

**2.3 Conditional expectation.** Recommended oracle model (matches `ky-hieu.md` $\xi$, $A(S;\xi)$): $g_t=\varphi(w_t,\xi_t)$ with $\xi_1,\xi_2,\ldots$ i.i.d., independent of $w_1$ (and of $S$), $\varphi$ jointly measurable; $\mathcal F_{t}=\sigma(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_t)$; $w_t$ is $\mathcal F_{t-1}$-measurable (induction: $\Pi_W$ is continuous). Assumptions become deterministic statements about $\psi(w)=\mathbb E\varphi(w,\xi)$: (O-u) $\psi(w)\in\partial F(w)$ for all $w\in W$; (O-m) $\mathbb E\lVert\varphi(w,\xi)\rVert^2\le G^2$ for all $w\in W$. By the independence (freezing) lemma, $\mathbb E[g_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\psi(w_t)$ and $\mathbb E[\lVert g_t\rVert^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le G^2$ a.s. — this is the blueprint's condition, now well posed. Key step: $\mathbb E\langle g_t,w_t-u\rangle=\mathbb E\,\mathbb E[\langle g_t,w_t-u\rangle\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\mathbb E\langle\psi(w_t),w_t-u\rangle\ge\mathbb E[F(w_t)-F(u)]$ (tower property; "taking out what is known" because $w_t-u$ is $\mathcal F_{t-1}$-measurable; subgradient inequality at $w_t$ with $u\in W$). Integrability: $\mathbb E\lVert g_t\rVert\le G$ and $\lVert w_t-u\rVert$ is bounded if $W$ is bounded; for unbounded $W$, $\mathbb E\lVert w_t-u\rVert^2<\infty$ by induction from 2.2 — say "all expectations are finite" in a footnote-level remark. **Well-posedness:** "$\mathbb E[g_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\in\partial F(w_t)$ a.s." is meaningful for any version (needs only $\mathbb E\lVert g_t\rVert<\infty$); no measurable selection is needed for the theorem. A measurable selection is needed only to *build* an oracle from $\partial\ell(\cdot,z)$; for hinge/squared loss the explicit formula is Borel. **Conditioning on $w_t$ suffices** (UML's version), and the unconditional $\mathbb E\lVert g_t\rVert^2\le G^2$ suffices too. UML's inference p. 193 fails in general: $f\equiv0$ on $\mathbb R$, $v_1=\varepsilon_1$, $v_2=\varepsilon_2$ fair signs, $v_3=\varepsilon_1$ on $\{w_3=0\}$ (i.e. $\varepsilon_1=-\varepsilon_2$), fresh sign otherwise: $\mathbb E[v_3\mid w_3]=0\in\partial f$ but $\mathbb E[v_3\mid v_{1:2}]=\varepsilon_1\notin\partial f(w_3)=\{0\}$.

**2.4 Telescoping, Jensen, step size.** Sum 2.2 over $t$, take expectations, use 2.3 and (O-m): $\sum_t\mathbb E[F(w_t)-F(u)]\le\lVert w_1-u\rVert^2/(2\eta)+\eta TG^2/2$ (dropped $-\mathbb E\lVert w_{T+1}-u\rVert^2\le0$). $w_t\in W$ for $t\ge2$; need $w_1\in W$ so $\bar w_T\in W$ and $F(w_1)$, $\partial F(w_1)$ make sense. "Jensen" is the finite convexity inequality $F(\bar w_T)\le\frac1T\sum F(w_t)$, applied **pathwise** before expectation (no probabilistic Jensen). Result: $\mathbb E F(\bar w_T)-F(u)\le\lVert w_1-u\rVert^2/(2\eta T)+\eta G^2/2$. Minimise $a/\eta+b\eta$ ($a,b>0$): $\eta^*=\sqrt{a/b}$, value $2\sqrt{ab}$; here $a=R^2/(2T)$, $b=G^2/2$: $\eta^*=R/(G\sqrt T)$, bound $RG/\sqrt T$ ✓; $T\ge R^2G^2/\epsilon^2$ ✓. Robustness: $\eta=c\eta^*$ gives $\frac{RG}{\sqrt T}\cdot\frac{c+1/c}2$: $c=2$ or $1/2$ → ×1.25; $c=10$ → ×5.05.

**2.5 Comparator and minimiser.** The bound holds for **every** $u\in W$; no minimiser needed. Taking $\inf$ over $u\in W$ with $\lVert w_1-u\rVert\le R$ bounds $\mathbb EF(\bar w_T)-\inf\{F(u):u\in W,\lVert u-w_1\rVert\le R\}$. If $W$ has diameter $D_W$ ($w_1\in W$), $R=D_W$ works for all $u$ and the comparator is $\inf_WF$ (attained anyway when $W$ compact, $F$ continuous). With several minimisers, $R$ may be the distance to the nearest one. Recommend: theorem with comparator $u\in W$, corollary with $u=w^*$; this also matches Lecture 01's "$\inf$ not $\min$" rule. Note $F(\bar w_T)-F(u)$ may be negative for non-minimising $u$; Markov (§4) needs $u$ = minimiser or $\inf_W F$.

**2.6 GD as special case.** $g_t\in\partial F(w_t)$ deterministic: (O-u) trivial, (O-m) needs $\lVert g_t\rVert\le G$. If $F$ is convex and $G$-Lipschitz on an **open** set containing $W$, every (non-relative) subgradient has norm $\le G$ (Lemma 14.7). On the boundary, *relative* subgradients can be huge: $F\equiv0$ on $W=[0,1]$, every $v\le0$ is a relative subgradient at $0$. So assume $F$ finite convex on an open set $\supset W$ (or on $\mathbb R^p$) and the oracle returns one of its subgradients. Bound: $F(\bar w_T)-F(u)\le RG/\sqrt T$ deterministically; cost $m$ example-gradients per step when $F=L_S$.

**2.7 Mini-batch (Prop 5.5).** $g_t=\frac1b\sum_{j=1}^b\varphi(w_t,\xi_{t,j})$, $\xi_{t,j}$ i.i.d. (conditionally independent given $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ with common conditional mean $\psi(w_t)$). Conditional variance $\mathbb E[\lVert g_t-\psi(w_t)\rVert^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\frac1{b^2}\sum_j\mathbb E[\lVert\varphi_j-\psi\rVert^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le\sigma^2/b$ (cross terms vanish by conditional independence and zero conditional mean; uses only pairwise uncorrelatedness). Not identically distributed: $\frac1{b^2}\sum\sigma_j^2\le\max\sigma_j^2/b$. **Link to Theorem 5.3 (blueprint silent):** the theorem uses the second moment $G^2$, and $\mathbb E[\lVert g\rVert^2\mid\mathcal F]=\lVert\psi(w)\rVert^2+\mathrm{Var}$, so $G_b^2=G_F^2+\sigma^2/b$ with $\lVert\psi\rVert\le G_F$: only the variance part shrinks. Correlated draws: equicorrelation $\rho_c$ gives $\sigma^2(1+(b-1)\rho_c)/b\ge\rho_c\sigma^2$ (factor $0.625$ at $b=4$, $0.531$ at $b=16$ for $\rho_c=0.5$; factor $1$ for duplicated examples, $\rho_c=1$). Concrete correlated case: a batch of $b$ copies of one sampled example ("data augmentation without new information") has variance $\sigma^2$. Negative correlation helps: sampling without replacement from $S$ gives factor $(m-b)/(m-1)$ (toy $m=4$, values $1,2,4,9$, $b=2$: $\sigma^2=9.5$, with replacement $4.75$, without $3.1667=4.75\cdot\frac23$, enumerated exactly); $b=m$ without replacement = full gradient, variance 0. Also: random reshuffling per epoch is **not** conditionally unbiased given the history (within an epoch the next index is uniform over the *remaining* examples; $m=2$: second step is determined) — outside Theorem 5.3.

**2.8 Time-varying $\eta_t$.** From 2.2 with $\eta_t$: (a) weighted average $\tilde w=\sum\eta_tw_t/\sum\eta_t$: $\mathbb EF(\tilde w)-F(u)\le(R^2+G^2\sum\eta_t^2)/(2\sum\eta_t)$, no bounded $W$ needed; $\eta_t=R/(G\sqrt t)$ gives a $\ln T$ factor ($0.228$ at $T=1600$, $R=1$, $G=4$, vs $0.1$ for constant step). (b) uniform average, $\eta_t$ nonincreasing, $W$ of diameter $D_W$: Abel summation gives $\frac1T[D_W^2/(2\eta_T)+\frac{G^2}2\sum\eta_t]$; $\eta_t=D_W/(G\sqrt t)$ and $\sum_{t\le T}t^{-1/2}\le2\sqrt T-1$ give $\le\frac{3D_WG}{2\sqrt T}$ (checked: $T=1600$, $D_W=2$, $G=4$: $0.2964\le0.3$). Needs $\lVert w_t-u\rVert\le D_W$ for all $t$, i.e. projection onto a bounded $W$; UML Ex. 14.4 ("analog of Thm 14.8", which has no projection) is therefore under-specified. Advantage: anytime (no $T$ in $\eta_t$). **Recommendation:** note uses constant $\eta$ only (write $\eta_t\equiv\eta$ once, or amend `ky-hieu.md`, §6); variable steps as a remark + exercise (UML 14.4 with projection added).

**2.9 Expectation vs high probability; average vs last.** Theorem 5.3 is an expectation over $\xi$ (and over $S$ if random); the last iterate is not covered (counterexample (iii)). Markov conversion in §4.

## 3. Worked examples and counterexamples (numbers verified)

**(a) Projected SGD, bounded-data regression** (`l05a`). $p=2$, $S$: $x_1=(1,0),y_1=1$; $x_2=(0,1),y_2=-1$; $x_3=(0.6,0.8),y_3=0$; squared loss $\ell(w,(x,y))=(\langle w,x\rangle-y)^2$, $F=L_S$, $W=\{\lVert w\rVert\le B\}$, $B=1$, $w_1=0$, sampled indices $i_1=1,i_2=2$, $\eta=0.5$ (illustration; not $\eta^*$). Gradient $2(\langle w,x\rangle-y)x$; $\lVert x\rVert\le C_x=1$, $\lvert y\rvert\le C_y=1$ ⇒ $\lVert g\rVert\le2(BC_x+C_y)C_x=G=4$ on $W$ (bound fails off $W$: grows like $\lVert w\rVert$). $L_S(w_1)=0.6667$. Step 1: $g=(-2,0)$, $w_1-\eta g=(1,0)$ (on the sphere, projection inactive), $L_S=0.4533$. Step 2: $g=(0,2)$, $w_2-\eta g=(1,-1)$, norm $1.4142>1$, projection $(0.7071,-0.7071)$, $L_S=0.0639$. $\min_WL_S=0.06112$ at $(0.7473,-0.6645)$ (boundary; unconstrained LS solution $(1.06,-0.92)$, norm $1.404$, $L_S=0.00667$) — shows the comparator is the constrained minimum. $R=\lVert w_1-w^*\rVert=1$. Actual $\sup_W\mathbb E_i\lVert g\rVert^2=7.85$ (vs $G^2=16$), $\sup_W\lVert\nabla L_S\rVert=1.81$: the worst-case $G$ is conservative.

**(b) Numerical bound** (`l05b`). $R=1,G=4$: $T\ge R^2G^2/\epsilon^2=400,1600,6400$ for $\epsilon=0.2,0.1,0.05$; $\eta^*=1/(4\sqrt T)$, at $T=1600$: $\eta^*=0.00625$. (For example (a), an informal Monte-Carlo check gave mean gap $0.022\le0.1$; not for the note — empirical curves are not proofs, §E.) Full-batch vs SGD vs mini-batch with $G_F^2=1$, $\sigma^2=15$ ($G^2=16$ at $b=1$), $\epsilon=0.1$, $m=10^4$: $b=1$: $T=1600$, 1600 examples; $b=4$: $T=475$, 1900; $b=16$: $T=194$, 3104; $b=64$: $T=124$, 7936; $b=256$: $T=106$, 27136; full-batch GD ($\sigma^2=0$): $T=100$, $10^6$ examples. Examples processed $=R^2(bG_F^2+\sigma^2)/\epsilon^2$ increases with $b$; updates decrease until $b\approx\sigma^2/G_F^2$. Caveat: comparison is within the Lipschitz model; faster GD rates under smoothness are not in the assigned sources — do not quote them without a source.

**(c) Hinge subgradient** (`l05b`). $x=(1,2)$, $\tilde y=+1$, $\ell(w)=\max\{0,1-\tilde y\langle w,x\rangle\}$ (max of two affine maps ⇒ convex). $w=0$: margin 0, $\ell=1$, $\partial\ell=\{-\tilde yx\}=\{(-1,-2)\}$. $w=(0.5,0.25)$: margin exactly 1 (kink), $\ell=0$, $\partial\ell=\{-\alpha\tilde yx:\alpha\in[0,1]\}$ (segment from $0$ to $(-1,-2)$); checked numerically that $\alpha\in[0,1]$ satisfy the subgradient inequality and $\alpha=1.2$ violates it. $w=(1,1)$: margin 3, $\partial\ell=\{0\}$. Lipschitz constant $\lVert x\rVert=\sqrt5=2.236$. Labels: course $y\in\{0,1\}$, $\tilde y=2y-1$; $\ell_{0\text{-}1}(h_{w,0},z)\le\mathbb 1[\tilde y\langle w,x\rangle\le0]\le$ hinge (course's $\ge0$ convention only helps).

**Remove-an-assumption counterexamples** (`l05c`):
- **(i) Nonconvex.** $f(w)=\min\{(w-1)^2,(w+1)^2+0.5\}$ (branches switch at $w=-0.125$); GD, $\eta=0.25$, $w_1=-1.5$: $w_{t+1}+1=\frac12(w_t+1)$: $-1.25,-1.125,-1.0625,\ldots\to-1$, $f\to0.5$, global min $0$ at $w=1$. Simpler: $f(w)=(w^2-1)^2$, $w_1=0$: $f'(0)=0$, GD stays, $f=1$ vs $0$. Proof breaks at the subgradient inequality. (UML Ex. 12.1 gives the 0–1 version.)
- **(ii) Unbounded gradients / step too large.** GD on $\frac a2w^2$: $w_{t+1}=(1-\eta a)w_t$, diverges iff $\eta>2/a$ ($a=8$: $\eta=0.2$ factor $-0.6$; $0.25$ factor $-1$, oscillates; $0.3$ factor $-1.4$, $\lvert w_{11}\rvert=28.9$ from $w_1=1$). Single squared-loss point $x=2$: $a=2x^2=8$. SGD vs GD: $X$ uniform on $\{1,3\}$, $Y=0$, $F(w)=\mathbb E(wX)^2=5w^2$, GD stable iff $\eta<0.2$; at $\eta=0.15$ GD factor $-0.5$ converges, SGD factors $0.7$ / $-1.7$, $\mathbb E\ln\lvert\cdot\rvert=0.087>0$ (a.s. divergence), $\mathbb E[\text{factor}^2]=1.69$ (second moment explodes); simulation: 97% of runs have $\lvert w_{101}\rvert>1$. No finite $G$ on $\mathbb R$; projection onto a ball restores $G$ (example (a)).
- **(iii) Last vs average.** $f(w)=\lvert w\rvert$, GD, $\eta=0.2$, $w_1=0.1$: iterates $0.1,-0.1,0.1,\ldots$; $f(w_t)=0.1$ forever, $\bar w_T=0$ for even $T$. Honest wording: the last iterate does not converge with fixed $\eta$; it does not violate the averaged bound's floor $\eta G^2/2=0.1$ here. No simple hand example shows a last-iterate violation; last-iterate theory is [O]/out of scope.
- **(iv) Biased oracle.** $S$: $x=1$, $y\in\{0,1\}$, $L_S(w)=\frac12[w^2+(w-1)^2]$, min $0.5$; sampling $y=0$ w.p. $0.9$ makes the oracle unbiased for $0.9w^2+0.1(w-1)^2$, minimiser $0.1$, $L_S(0.1)-L_S(0.5)=0.16$ for every $T$. Constant bias $c$ on $\frac12w^2$: limit $-c$, gap $c^2/2$ ($0.125$ at $c=0.5$). Also: random reshuffling, gradient clipping, reuse of $S$ for $L_D$.
- **(v) Error floor (blueprint ex. 6).** $F(w)=\frac a2w^2$, $g_t=aw_t+\nu_t$, $\nu_t$ i.i.d. mean 0 variance $\sigma^2$, $0<\eta<2/a$: $e_{t+1}=(1-\eta a)e_t-\eta\nu_t$, $v=\mathbb Ee_t^2$ satisfies $v_{t+1}=(1-\eta a)^2v_t+\eta^2\sigma^2$, stationary $v_\infty=\eta\sigma^2/(a(2-\eta a))$, $\mathbb EF-F^*\to\eta\sigma^2/(2(2-\eta a))$. $a=\sigma^2=1$: $\eta=0.1,0.05,0.01$ → $v_\infty=0.05263,0.02564,0.005025$; gap $0.02632,0.01282,0.002513$ (≈ $\eta\sigma^2/4$, proportional to $\eta$). Transient: $v_t=(1-\eta a)^{2(t-1)}e_1^2+v_\infty(1-(1-\eta a)^{2(t-1)})$. Averaging removes the floor: exact $\mathbb E\bar w_T^2=0.000496$ at $T=2000$, $\eta=0.1$, $w_1=0$ (≈ $\sigma^2/(a^2T)=0.0005$). Good combined exercise for (iii)+(v).

## 4. Relation to ERM and the course story

- **What the oracle estimates.** Given $S$, $\xi_t=i_t$ uniform on $\{1..m\}$ **with replacement**, independent of the past and of $S$: $\psi(w)=\frac1m\sum_iv(w,z_i)\in\partial L_S(w)$ (only the easy direction of the sum rule: a sum of subgradients is a subgradient). So Theorem 5.3 applies to $F=L_S$ conditionally on $S$: $\mathbb E_\xi[L_S(\bar w_T)]-\inf_WL_S\le RG/\sqrt T$, uniformly in $S$ if $W=\bar B(0,B)$, $w_1=0$ ($R\le B$) and $G$ bounds subgradients for all $w\in W$ and all $z$ in the support. Fresh i.i.d. $Z_t\sim D$ per step: $\psi\in\partial L_D$ (UML p. 197, interchange of expectation and subgradient inequality) ⇒ population bound = UML Cor. 14.12 = **Lecture 06**. Reusing $S$: at $t\ge2$, $w_t$ depends on $S$, so $\mathbb E[g_t\mid w_t]\ne\nabla L_D(w_t)$ in general. Extreme case for the bridge: $m=1$, $x=1$, $Y=\pm1$ fair, $W=[-1,1]$: SGD on $S=\{(1,1)\}$ converges toward $w=1$ with $L_D(1)=2$, while $\min L_D=L_D(0)=1$.
- **Lecture 05 may say:** the bound is an optimisation guarantee for whatever objective the oracle is unbiased for; with sampling from $S$ it bounds the *expected empirical optimisation error* $\mathbb E_\xi\operatorname{opt}_S(\bar w_T)$ (Lecture 04's $\operatorname{opt}_S$, now for a convex loss on $W$). **Must wait:** population risk of SGD (Lecture 06), generalisation of a convex surrogate class (needs Rademacher, Lecture 07, or stability, Lecture 06), and any 0–1 statement from hinge SGD (surrogate gap).
- **Markov conversion.** $\operatorname{opt}_S(\bar w_T)=L_S(\bar w_T)-\inf_WL_S\ge0$ (needs $\bar w_T\in W$). For each $S$: $\Pr_\xi[\operatorname{opt}_S(\bar w_T)>\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}]\le RG/(\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\sqrt T)$, so $T\ge R^2G^2/(\delta'^2\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}^2)$ gives Lecture 01's (O2) only *with probability $\ge1-\delta'$ over $\xi$* — not the deterministic "for every $S$" of the $\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$-ERM definition (Lecture 01 line 464). $R=1,G=4,\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}=0.1,\delta'=0.05$: $T=640\,000$ vs $1600$ in expectation (factor $1/\delta'^2=400$). Confidence boosting (own, elementary): $k$ independent runs at expected level $\epsilon/2$ ($T=6400$ each), keep the run with smallest exact $L_S$; failure $\le2^{-k}$ by Markov at level $\epsilon$; $k=5$ gives $0.03125\le0.05$: $32\,000$ steps $+\,5m$ loss evaluations. Combining with Lecture 04's bound (bounded loss and uniform convergence for the same loss): union bound over the $S$-event and the $\xi$-event, probability $\ge1-\delta-\delta'$ over $(S,\xi)$.

## 6. Risks and terminology

1. **Notation clashes** (`ky-hieu.md`): $\Pi_W$ (blueprint projection) vs growth function $\Pi_{\mathcal H}(m)$ — keep $\Pi_W$ only with subscript $W$, or use $\operatorname{proj}_W$ (Lecture 04 already banned $\pi$ and used $\mathrm{pr}_k$); decision for master. Bare constant $\eta$ vs $\eta(x)$ (table says step size "always indexed") — amend the table ("$\eta(x)$ always with argument; bare $\eta$ = constant step") or write $\eta_t\equiv\eta$. $\lambda$: regularisation vs strong-convexity modulus (UML uses $\lambda$ for both; $\frac\lambda2\lVert w\rVert^2$ is $\lambda$-strongly convex — fix the convention with Lecture 06); $\mu$ is taken (means, Lectures 01–03). $\beta$ (smoothness) vs $\beta(m,\delta)$ (Lecture 01) — tolerable, mention. $b$ (batch size) vs offset $b$ of $h_{w,b}$ (Lecture 03) — use homogeneous halfspaces in hinge examples. $g_t$ (stochastic gradient) vs candidates $g_j,\hat g$ (Lectures 02, 04). $\mathcal F_t$ (history) vs $\mathcal F$ function class of Lecture 07 — always subscripted. Gradient noise: use $\nu_t$ (not $\xi$ = internal randomness, not $\zeta_i$ = label noise of Lecture 04, not $\varepsilon$). UML's $\rho$ (Lipschitz) → $G$; UML's $B$ (radius) is free: use $B$ for the radius of $W$ and $C_x,C_y$ (or similar) for data bounds; UML's $R=\max\lVert x_i\rVert$ in Ex. 12.6.3/14.8.3 must be renamed. Parameters $w\in W\subset\mathbb R^p$ ($p$ feature dimension, not UML's $d$); relate $\mathcal H=\{h_w:w\in W\}$ and write $\ell(w,z)$ as UML Ch. 12 does.
2. **Measurability:** $\varphi$ jointly measurable; $w_t$ measurable by induction; $A(S;\xi)=\bar w_T$ extends Định nghĩa 01.4 (promise Lecture 01 line 629: probability over both $S$ and $\xi$).
3. **Blueprint statements to tighten:** (a) $F$ finite convex on an open set $\supset W$ (or subgradients relative to $W$ + bounded oracle), $w_1\in W$; (b) "minimiser exists" unnecessary — comparator any $u\in W$; (c) oracle as $\varphi(w_t,\xi_t)$ with i.i.d. $\xi_t$ makes "conditional mean in $\partial F(w_t)$" concrete; conditional second moment can be relaxed to $\mathbb E\lVert g_t\rVert^2\le G^2$; (d) Prop 5.5 needs a common conditional mean and must be linked to $G^2=\lVert\psi\rVert^2+\mathrm{Var}$ (variance /b, not $G^2/b$); (e) Corollary 5.4's $\eta$ depends on $T$; (f) "Jensen" = finite convexity inequality pathwise; (g) sampling from $S$ must be with replacement (reshuffling is outside the theorem).
4. **Course standards:** state probability space ($\xi$ only, $S$ fixed; or joint), guarantee type (expectation, optimisation gap of $F$, algorithm-level), dependence on $T,R,G$, none on $m$ or $p$ explicitly (through $G$, $R$). Squared loss: only on bounded $W$ with bounded data (blueprint §5.4). Don't claim convex results for networks.
