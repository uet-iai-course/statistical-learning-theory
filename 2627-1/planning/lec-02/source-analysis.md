## 2. Delicate points the note must handle

1. **Measurability.** Finite $\mathcal H$: $\max_h\lvert L_S(h)-L_D(h)\rvert$ is a maximum of finitely many measurable functions; ERM with a fixed tie-breaking order takes finitely many values on measurable sets (Boolean combinations of $\{L_S(h)\le L_S(h')\}$). This discharges Lecture 01 Phụ lục A(v) for finite classes; infinite classes are Lecture 3's job.
2. **Where independence enters.** Only in the MGF factorisation $\mathbb E\prod_ie^{tX_i}=\prod_i\mathbb Ee^{tX_i}$ (Hoeffding) and the product $(1-L_D(h))^m$ (realizable). Chebyshev needs only pairwise uncorrelatedness. Unbiasedness (Lecture 01 Mệnh đề 01.7) needs neither. **Not used:** in the union bound, and not between the events of different hypotheses (they are computed on the same sample and are strongly dependent; subadditivity holds for any events).
3. **Where boundedness enters.** Only in Hoeffding's lemma (range $b-a$). The rate scales with $(b-a)^2$; unbounded losses (squared, log) have no distribution-free Hoeffding bound (§4 counterexample b).
4. **Union bound needs no independence** and is tight-ish only when the bad events are nearly disjoint; when hypotheses are similar (thresholds) it is loose — compare 48 vs 30 vs 14 in §4.
5. **"Simultaneously for all $h$" vs "for each $h$".** $\forall h\,\Pr[E_h]\ge1-\delta$ does not give $\Pr[\cap_hE_h]\ge1-\delta$; ERM needs the latter because $\hat h$ is chosen after seeing $S$. Exam 1 focus (blueprint §C): "distinguish fixed and data-selected hypotheses".
6. **Data-dependent class.** The union bound is over a class fixed before the sample that is used for evaluation. Choosing the class (or the candidate list) after looking at that sample voids it. Remedy: fix in advance a countable family with weights and split $\delta$ (blueprint exercise 6, prepares SRM in Lecture 4), or use a fresh sample.
7. **Validation reuse (opening question).** Candidates trained on $S$ and evaluated on an independent validation sample $V$ of size $m_V$: conditional on $S$ the $K$ candidates are fixed, so Theorem 2.3 applies on $V$ with $\lvert\mathcal H\rvert=K$ and cost $\ln K$. If later candidates are designed after looking at $V$'s scores (adaptive reuse), the list depends on $V$ and the bound no longer applies.
8. **Agnostic $1/\epsilon^2$ vs realizable $1/\epsilon$.** Realizable: the only event to exclude is "a hypothesis with risk $>\epsilon$ makes zero errors", probability $(1-\epsilon)^m$, a small-variance regime (Bernoulli near 0). Agnostic: risks may be near $1/2$, variance up to $1/4$, deviations $\asymp1/\sqrt m$. Bernstein (UML B.5.1, p. 427) interpolates; [O] only. The note must not claim at this point that $1/\epsilon^2$ is *necessary* (lower bounds are later, Lecture 3/UML Ch. 6).
9. **One-sided vs two-sided.** A risk *upper* confidence bound needs only one tail: $\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)}$ instead of $\sqrt{\ln(2/\delta)/(2m)}$.
10. **The three uses of $\epsilon$ are different** (estimation accuracy $\pm\epsilon$; excess over best in class; absolute risk with comparator 0) — see §4 fairness note.
11. **Weighted loss.** Lecture 01's $\ell_c\in\{0,1,4\}$ has range $4$: every Hoeffding-based sample size gets the factor $(b-a)^2=16$ (UML Ex. 4.2). Realizable Prop. 2.5 is 0–1 only.

## 3. UML motivation and order; what to borrow

UML order: Ch. 2 proves the realizable finite-class result first (§2.3.1, with misleading samples and the union bound), *before* any concentration inequality; Ch. 4 then motivates uniform convergence from the ERM inequality (Lemma 4.2 first, then Def. 4.3, Cor. 4.4), and only then proves finite-class UC by union bound + Hoeffding, citing App. B for the proof. App. B is a stand-alone toolbox (Markov → Chebyshev → Chernoff → Hoeffding → Bernstein).

Borrow (structure only): (i) the *reduction* direction of Ch. 4 — "if the sample is $\epsilon/2$-representative, every ERM is good" — stated as a deterministic implication, then the probabilistic part; (ii) the two-step pattern "union bound, then per-hypothesis tail"; (iii) the misleading-sample picture (bad set $\mathcal H_B$; can be an SVG of overlapping bad-sample regions, redrawn); (iv) App. B's progression Markov → Chebyshev (polynomial tails) → exponential tails, with the explicit comparison of $1/\delta$ vs $\ln(1/\delta)$.

Avoid: UML's i.i.d. hypothesis in Hoeffding (blueprint is more general and correct); the $(D,f)$ labelling-function notation; UML's $\delta$/$h$ reuse in B.3; the "discretization trick" remark (p. 57–58, $2^{64d}$) — tempting but it ties the bound to machine representation; at most a one-line pointer, better left to Lecture 3's motivation. UML puts realizable before agnostic; the blueprint puts agnostic first (UC + ERM), then realizable as a sharper special case — keep the blueprint's order (it matches the course's joint-$D$ framing and Lecture 01's ending).

**Opening question (validation error 5%, $\delta=0.05$).** Two-sided Hoeffding radius $r=\sqrt{\ln(2K/\delta)/(2m)}$:

One-sided upper bounds: $\sqrt{\ln(K/\delta)/(2m)}$ = 0.0387 / 0.0704 / 0.0759 at $m=1000$. The tempting wrong answer: report the fixed-$h$ interval after selection. Illustration that selection really biases (constructed, exact binomial): $K$ candidates whose validation losses are independent Bernoulli(0.07) across candidates and examples (e.g. $X\in\{0,1\}^K$ with independent coordinates of mean $0.07$, $Y=0$, $h_k(x)=x_k$); $\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(1000,0.07)\le50]=0.00593$, so $\Pr[\text{some candidate shows}\le5\%]=1-(1-0.00593)^{1000}=0.9974$, while every candidate has true error $7\%$ (outside a naive normal-approximation interval $0.05\pm1.96\sqrt{0.0475/1000}=0.05\pm0.0135$, inside the honest $K=1000$ bound). With true error $0.10$ instead: $\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}\le50]=6.0\times10^{-9}$, so even $K=5000$ gives only $3.0\times10^{-5}$ — consistent with the UC bound. Independence across candidates is a worst-case-like construction, not a claim about real models.

**"Remove an assumption" counterexamples (proposals, numbers re-derived).**
- (a) *Drop independence.* $Z_1=\dots=Z_m=Z\sim D_\eta$ (one patient record copied $m$ times). Then $L_S(h_3)=\ell(h_3,Z)\in\{0,1\}$, so $\lvert L_S(h_3)-0.22\rvert\ge0.22>0.2$ with probability 1 for every $m$, while Hoeffding would give $2e^{-2m(0.04)}=6.7\times10^{-4}$ at $m=100$. Unbiasedness still holds ($\mathbb EL_S(h_3)=0.22$), continuing Lecture 01's quick-review question 2. **Recommended** (uses the running example).
- (b) *Drop boundedness.* Losses $\theta_i\in\{0,M\}$ i.i.d. with $\Pr[\theta_i=M]=1/M$, mean 1; $M=m^2$. $\Pr[L_S=0]=(1-1/m^2)^m\ge1-1/m$; at $m=100$, $M=10^4$: $(1-10^{-4})^{100}=e^{100\ln(0.9999)}=0.9900$. So $\lvert L_S-L_D\rvert=1$ with probability $0.99$; no bound of the form $c\,e^{-cm\epsilon^2}$ holds uniformly over unbounded nonnegative losses with mean 1. (Squared loss with a rare huge label.)
- (c) *Drop $\lvert\mathcal H\rvert$ from the union bound* (blueprint exercise 4). $Y=0$, $X\in\{0,1\}^K$ uniform, $h_k(x)=x_k$: all $L_D(h_k)=1/2$, losses independent fair coins. $m=10$: $\Pr[\exists k:L_S(h_k)=0]=1-(1-2^{-10})^K=0.6236$ ($K=1000$), $0.9924$ ($K=5000$), each such event a deviation $1/2>0.4$; the $\lvert\mathcal H\rvert$-free claim $2e^{-2\cdot10\cdot0.16}=2e^{-3.2}=0.0815$ fails. With the factor $K$ the bound is $\ge1$, so no contradiction.
