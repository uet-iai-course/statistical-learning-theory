## 2. Proofs and constants (all re-derived)

### 2.1 Prop 4.1
Setting: $\ell\in[0,1]$ (or bounded below so $\inf_{\mathcal H}L_S>-\infty$); $\tilde h=\tilde h(S)\in\mathcal H$ any output (any rule); $\operatorname{opt}_S(\tilde h)=L_S(\tilde h)-\inf_{\mathcal H}L_S\ge0$; $L^*\in\mathbb R$ any constant. Event $E_r=\{\sup_{h\in\mathcal H}\lvert L_D(h)-L_S(h)\rvert\le r\}$ ("$S$ is $r$-đại diện", Lecture 02 term), measurable under (M). **Deterministic on $E_r$:** for every $h\in\mathcal H$, $L_D(\tilde h)\le L_S(\tilde h)+r=\inf_{\mathcal H}L_S+\operatorname{opt}_S(\tilde h)+r\le L_S(h)+\operatorname{opt}_S(\tilde h)+r\le L_D(h)+2r+\operatorname{opt}_S(\tilde h)$; take $\inf_h$, subtract $L^*$. Uses: deviation at the random $\tilde h$ (this is why the deviation must be uniform), deviation at every competitor $h$ (a fixed $h$ would suffice per $h$, but the $\inf$ needs all, or a fixed near-minimising sequence). **Where inf vs min matters:** $\inf_{\mathcal H}L_S$ may be unattained (Lecture 01 Ví dụ 01.7, $\mathcal H_+$, $w^2$): $\operatorname{opt}_S$ is still defined; $\inf_{\mathcal H}L_D$ may be unattained: take $\inf$ at the end. **$L^*$:** cancels, so the inequality holds for every real $L^*$; the label "approximation error" (nonnegative) requires $L^*\le\inf_{\mathcal H}L_D$, e.g. $L^*=L_D^{\mathrm{Bayes}}$ (classification; Lecture 01 identity after Định nghĩa 01.24) or $L^*=\inf$ over a larger reference class (e.g. the union in SRM). Probability enters only via $\Pr(E_r)\ge1-\delta$ from Thm 02.14(a) ($r=r_m$) or Thm 03.15(a) ($r=\rho_m$). **Not an identity:** the exact identity is $L_D(\tilde h)-L^*=[\inf_{\mathcal H}L_D-L^*]+[L_D(\tilde h)-\inf_{\mathcal H}L_D]$; the second term contains estimation *and* optimisation effects; Prop 4.1 bounds it by $2r+\operatorname{opt}_S$. A population "optimisation error" $L_D(\tilde h)-L_D(\hat h_{\mathrm{ERM}})$ is ill-defined under ties and **can be negative** (an inexact optimiser may generalise better); $\operatorname{opt}_S\ge0$ is the empirical quantity. Ví dụ: D_η, thresholds, $h_2$: $0.32-0.18=0.04+0.10$ (Lecture 01 numbers; reusing $D_\eta$ needs a `ky-hieu.md` amendment, which limits it to Lectures 01–02).

### 2.2 Prop 4.2
Exact assumptions: fixed $x$; $\hat f_S(x)$ real, $\mathbb E_S\hat f_S(x)^2<\infty$; test response $Y$ with $\mathbb E[Y]=f^*(x)$, $\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\sigma^2(x)<\infty$ (law of $Y$ given $X=x$), **$Y$ independent of $S$** (uncorrelated with $\hat f_S(x)$ suffices). $S$ need not be i.i.d. Expectation is over the joint law of $(S,Y)$ = product. Write $\bar f=\mathbb E_S\hat f_S(x)$: $\hat f-Y=(\hat f-\bar f)+(\bar f-f^*)+(f^*-Y)$. Cross terms: $\mathbb E[(\hat f-\bar f)](\bar f-f^*)=0$ (constant factor); $(\bar f-f^*)\mathbb E[f^*-Y]=0$; $\mathbb E[(\hat f-\bar f)(f^*-Y)]=\mathbb E[\hat f-\bar f]\,\mathbb E[f^*-Y]=0$ — **only this one uses independence**. Cauchy–Schwarz makes all products integrable. Integrating over a test $X\sim D_{\mathcal X}$ independent of $S$ (Fubini) gives $\mathbb E_SL_D(\hat f_S)=\mathbb E_X[\text{bias}^2+\text{var}+\sigma^2](X)$ for squared loss: an **expectation** statement about one **algorithm**, not a class guarantee or high-probability bound. $\mathbb E\sigma^2(X)$ is the Bayes risk for squared loss ($f^*=\mathbb E[Y\mid X=\cdot]$). **Bias ≠ approximation error:** $\bar f$ need not lie in $\mathcal H$. Example: $f^*\equiv0$, $\mathcal H=\{\pm1\text{ constants}\}$, ERM $=\operatorname{sign}$ of $\bar Y$ with symmetric noise: $\bar f=0$, bias $0$, variance $1$, while the approximation error is $\inf_{\mathcal H}\mathbb E(h-Y)^2-\sigma^2=1$.

### 2.3 Lemma 4.3
Requirement on the radius: $\mathrm{pen}_k(m,\delta')$ is a **deterministic** function of $(k,m,\delta')$, fixed before data, valid for **every** $D$: $\Pr_{S\sim D^m}[\sup_{\mathcal H_k}\lvert L_D-L_S\rvert>\mathrm{pen}_k(m,\delta')]\le\delta'$, two-sided (Thm 4.4 uses both sides). Countable union bound: failure $\le\sum_k\delta q_k\le\delta$. Concrete radii: finite $\mathcal H_k$: $\sqrt{\ln(2\lvert\mathcal H_k\rvert/(\delta q_k))/(2m)}$ (Thm 02.14(a)); VC: $\rho^{(k)}_m(\delta q_k)=\sqrt{8\ln(4\Pi_{\mathcal H_k}(2m)/(\delta q_k))/m}\le\sqrt{8(d_k\ln(2em/d_k)+\ln(4/\delta)+\ln(1/q_k))/m}$ for $1\le d_k\le m$ (Thm 03.15). $\sum6/(\pi^2k^2)=1$, $\ln(1/q_k)=2\ln k+\ln(\pi^2/6)=2\ln k+0.4977$; $q_k=2^{-k}$: $k\ln2$. Values of $\ln(1/q_k)$, $k=1..10$: $\pi$-weights $0.4977,1.884,2.695,3.270,3.717,4.081,4.390,4.657,4.892,5.103$; $2^{-k}$: $0.6931,1.386,2.079,2.773,3.466,4.159,4.852,5.545,6.238,6.931$; $2^{-k}$ is smaller for $k=2..5$, larger from $k=6$; $1/q_k$ grows polynomially vs exponentially, so $2^{-k}$ penalises long lists much more. Finite list of $K$ classes: $q_k=1/K$ allowed. Example of the cheapness of the split: at $m=10^4$, $\delta=0.05$, $d=3$: $\rho$ goes $0.1644\to0.1690$ ($q_2$); $d=11$: $0.2799\to0.2871$ ($q_{10}$).

### 2.4 Theorem 4.4
On the Lemma 4.3 event $E$: for all $k$ and $h\in\mathcal H_k$, $L_D(\hat h)\le L_S(\hat h)+\mathrm{pen}_{\hat k}\le L_S(h)+\mathrm{pen}_k+\tau\le L_D(h)+2\mathrm{pen}_k+\tau$, where **$\tau$-accuracy means** $\hat h\in\mathcal H_{\hat k}$ and $L_S(\hat h)+\mathrm{pen}_{\hat k}\le\inf_k\inf_{h\in\mathcal H_k}[L_S(h)+\mathrm{pen}_k]+\tau$. Taking $\inf_h$ then $\inf_k$: $L_D(\hat h)\le\inf_k[\inf_{\mathcal H_k}L_D+2\mathrm{pen}_k]+\tau$, **$\tau$ additive with coefficient 1**, outside the infimum. **Nestedness:** not used anywhere. **Attainment:** neither $\inf_{\mathcal H_k}L_D$ nor $\inf_k$ need be attained (bound is stated with $\inf$, one event for all $k$). Existence of an exact penalised minimiser is not automatic: for 0–1 loss $\min_{h\in\mathcal H_k}L_S$ exists; if $\mathrm{pen}_k\to\infty$ (true for unclipped VC/finite radii, since $\ln(1/q_k)\to\infty$) only finitely many $k$ can beat $k=1$, so the minimum exists; in general (e.g. clipped penalties, degenerate classes) use $\tau>0$, which always exists since the objective is $\ge0$. **Classwise-ERM-then-select:** if $\hat h_k$ is an $\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$-ERM on $\mathcal H_k$ for every $k$ (uniform $\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$) and $\hat k$ is a $\tau'$-minimiser of $L_S(\hat h_k)+\mathrm{pen}_k$, then $\tau=\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}+\tau'$; more finely $L_D(\hat h_{\hat k})\le\inf_k[\inf_{\mathcal H_k}L_D+2\mathrm{pen}_k+\operatorname{opt}^{(k)}_S(\hat h_k)]+\tau'$ (the gap of the *competitor* class appears, not of the selected one). With countably many classes this needs a stopping rule: if $\mathrm{pen}_k$ is nondecreasing, stop once $\mathrm{pen}_k\ge$ best value so far. Penalty must also be **independent of the sample and of $h$** within the class; a data-dependent penalty is not covered. Price of not knowing $k$: $\ln(1/q_k)$ inside the radius (§4a).

### 2.5 Nonuniform learnability from Thm 4.4
For $h\in\mathcal H_k$ (any $k$ containing it): $L_D(\hat h)\le L_D(h)+2\mathrm{pen}_k(m,\delta q_k)$, so $m^{NUL}(\epsilon,\delta,h)\le$ the least $m_0$ with $2\mathrm{pen}_k(m,\delta q_k)\le\epsilon$ for all $m\ge m_0$ = UML Thm 7.5 rate; with Lecture 03 Thm 03.15(c): $\lceil\frac{64}{\epsilon^2}(d_k\ln\frac{128}{\epsilon^2}+\ln\frac4{\delta q_k})\rceil$. **δ-gap fix (recommended):** run SRM with $\delta_m=1/m$ (any $\delta_m\to0$ with $\ln(1/\delta_m)/m\to0$). For $m\ge1/\delta$ the event has probability $\ge1-\delta_m\ge1-\delta$, and $\mathrm{pen}_k(m,q_k/m)\to0$ (VC radius $\sim\sqrt{(d_k+1)\ln m/m}$); so one $\delta$-free algorithm works with $m^{NUL}\le\max(\lceil1/\delta\rceil,M_k(\epsilon))$. Alternative: define nonuniform learnability for a family $(A_\delta)$ and say it differs from Định nghĩa 01.25. **Open decision for the master.** Necessity (Thm 7.2 "⇐") and Remark 7.2: [O], statement only.

### 2.6 Validation corollary
Setting: training data $T$ (any law); candidates $g_1..g_K$ are functions of $T$ (and of algorithm randomness independent of $V$); $K$ may depend on $T$ but not on $V$; $V\sim D^{m_v}$ **independent of $(T,\text{candidates})$**; loss in $[0,1]$; $\hat g$ = any $L_V$-minimiser (ties arbitrary, measurable). Conditional on $T$ the menu is a fixed finite class and $V\sim D^{m_v}$ (Nhận xét 01.9), so Thm 02.14(a) with $K$ gives $\Pr(\text{all }\lvert L_V-L_D\rvert\le r\mid T)\ge1-\delta$, $r=\sqrt{\ln(2K/\delta)/(2m_v)}$; on it $L_D(\hat g)\le L_V(\hat g)+r\le L_V(g^\star)+r\le L_D(g^\star)+2r$, $g^\star\in\arg\min_jL_D(g_j)$ (finite, attained; depends on $T$ only); average over $T$. Two-sided Hoeffding: $2Ke^{-2m_vr^2}=\delta$ — **factor 2 = two tails**, the outer 2 in $2r$ = upper deviation at $\hat g$ plus lower at $g^\star$. Comparator is the best **candidate** (random through $T$), not $\inf_{\mathcal H_k}L_D$. **Refinement (optional remark/exercise):** only $K$ upper tails plus one lower tail at $g^\star$ are needed: $(K+1)e^{-2m_vr^2}=\delta$, excess $\le2\sqrt{\ln((K+1)/\delta)/(2m_v)}$, never worse since $K+1\le2K$. Recommend keeping the blueprint's $\ln(2K/\delta)$ for continuity with Hệ quả 02.16. **Combined with training:** if $g_j$ is ERM on $\mathcal H_j$ from $m_t$ training points, union of a training event at $\delta/2$ ($\delta/(2K)$ per class) and the validation event at $\delta/2$ gives $L_D(\hat g)\le\min_j[\inf_{\mathcal H_j}L_D+2\rho^{(j)}_{m_t}(\delta/(2K))]+2\sqrt{\ln(4K/\delta)/(2m_v)}$ (my derivation, correct). Not covered: adaptive reuse, $K$ chosen after seeing $V$, cross-validation (folds overlap, §5f).

## 4. "Remove an assumption" counterexamples

- **(a) Collection chosen after the data.** $X\sim U[0,1]$, $Y\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.8)$ independent of $X$, 0–1 loss. $\mathcal H_1=\{h\equiv1\}$, $L_D=0.2$. A class built from $S$: $\mathcal H_S=\{\mathbb 1_{F_+}\}$, $F_+$ = training inputs with label 1 (a.s. distinct): $L_S=0$, $L_D=0.8$. Treating $\mathcal H_S$ as a fixed singleton with $q=1/2$ ($m=1000$: radius $\sqrt{\ln80/2000}=0.0468$) claims $L_D\le0.0468$; truth $0.8$. Extends Lecture 03 counterexample (b) and Lecture 01 memoriser.
- **(b) Penalty not from a valid bound.** Same $D$, $\mathcal H_2=\{\mathbb 1_F:F\subset[0,1]\text{ finite}\}$ (VC $\infty$). Any valid radius for $\mathcal H_2$ at confidence $<1$ is $\ge0.8$ (deviation of $\mathbb 1_{F_+}$ is $0.8$ surely). With an ad hoc $\mathrm{pen}_2=0.1$ and $\mathrm{pen}_1=0.0468$: SRM picks $\mathbb 1_{F_+}$ whenever $L_S(\equiv1)>0.0532$ (fraction of zeros $\approx0.2$, so w.p. $\approx1$), while Thm 4.4's right side is $\le0.2+2(0.0468)=0.2936$; truth $0.8$.
- **(c) Confidence not split ($\sum q_k>1$).** Reuse Lecture 02 Bài tập 02.4: $X\in\{0,1\}^K$ i.i.d. Bernoulli(1/2) coordinates, $Y=0$, singleton classes $\{h_k\}$, $h_k(x)=x_k$, $m=10$, all $q_k=1$, radius $\sqrt{\ln40/20}=0.4295<0.5$: $\Pr(\exists k:L_S(h_k)=0)=1-(1-2^{-10})^{1000}=0.6236$ ($K=5000$: 0.9924), so Lemma 4.3's conclusion fails w.p. $>0.62\gg0.05$. Countable version: with infinitely many such coordinates the failure probability is 1 (independent events, divergent sum).
- **(d) Validation reused adaptively / not independent.** Use $V$ as training data (or design a candidate from $V$): $\mathbb 1_{F_+(V)}$ has $L_V=0$, $L_D=0.8$, beyond $0.1131$. Repeated tuning: after $J$ rounds the effective menu is all candidates that could have been produced, which may be unbounded; the fixed-menu $K$ is wrong.
- **(e) Test point not independent of $S$.** $\hat f=\bar Y$, "test" $=Y_1$: $\mathbb E(\bar Y-Y_1)^2=\sigma^2(m-1)/m=0.75$ ($m=4$, $\sigma^2=1$), while the identity would give $\sigma^2(1+1/m)=1.25$; the gap $2\sigma^2/m=-2\operatorname{Cov}$ is the optimism of training error.
- **(f) Bias–variance and 0–1 loss (careful wording).** With predictions and labels in $\{0,1\}$, $(\hat f-Y)^2=\mathbb 1[\hat f\ne Y]$, so Prop 4.2 still holds numerically, but its "bias" uses $\mathbb E\hat f\in[0,1]$, not a classifier. Classifier-level reading fails: $\eta(x)=0.9$, $\hat f(x)=1$ w.p. $0.4$ (majority prediction $0$, wrong): error $0.58$; deterministic $\hat f\equiv0$ (same majority, zero variance): error $0.90$. More variance, less error: no "noise + bias + variance" with a nonnegative variance penalty. (Squared-loss check: $(0.4-0.9)^2+0.24+0.09=0.58$.) Do not write "the identity is false for 0–1 loss"; write "it does not give a bias–variance trade-off for classifiers".
- **(g) Cross-validation (UML Ex. 11.1):** parity learner, LOO estimate always $0$ or $1$, true error $1/2$; each fold point is independent of its fold predictor (estimate unbiased for $\mathbb E L_D(A(S_{m-1}))=1/2$) but the $m$ terms are perfectly dependent: concentration, not unbiasedness, fails.

## 6. Delicate points for the writer

1. **Notation:** blueprint $p_k$ clashes with $p$ (feature dimension; the SRM example uses $p=10$): use $\mathrm{pen}_k$ (or another non-$p$ symbol); weights $q_k$ (not UML's $w$, which is a halfspace weight); validation size $m_v$ (UML's; never $n$); $\tau$ vs $\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$: $\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$ is the per-class ERM tolerance (Lecture 01), $\tau$ the penalised-objective tolerance; keep both, define $\tau$ at first use. $L^*$ new symbol (add to `ky-hieu.md`). $\sigma^2(x)$ vs Lecture 03 signs $\sigma_i$ (proof-only there; acceptable, note it). $f^*$, $\hat f_S$: Lecture 01 uses $f$ for labelling functions; consider $h^*_{\mathrm{reg}}$ or keep $f^*$ with a line in the notation table. $k$ indexes classes; Lecture 03 used $k$ for set sizes in places.
2. **What is random:** in Lemma 4.3 / Thm 4.4 only $S$; $(\mathcal H_k)$, $q_k$, $\mathrm{pen}_k$, $\delta$ fixed before data. In validation: $T$ and $V$; guarantee conditional on $T$, comparator random. In Prop 4.2: $S$ and the test $Y$; $x$ fixed.
3. **Class vs algorithm:** Lemma 4.3 is class-level (every $h$ in every class); Thm 4.4 is for every (τ-accurate) SRM output; Prop 4.2 is about one estimator in expectation; nonuniform learnability is existential.
4. **Risk / excess risk / optimisation gap:** Thm 4.4 bounds the *risk* of $\hat h$ by a penalised benchmark (oracle inequality), not excess risk over $\inf_{\bigcup}L_D$ at no cost; $\operatorname{opt}_S$ is empirical and $\ge0$; never call bias "approximation error" (§2.2).
5. **Nonuniform vs PAC:** sample size depends on the competitor $h$ (through its class index), never on $D$; per-$h$ probability; countable union of finite-VC classes may have VC $\infty$ (polynomial threshold classifiers; or $\{\mathbb 1_F:F\subset\mathbb Q\text{ finite}\}$, countable, VC $\infty$, NUL via Bài tập 02.6 weights). δ-dependence of SRM: §2.5.
6. **Regularisation (Lecture 06):** SRM penalises a *class index* with a bound-derived penalty; regularised ERM adds $\lambda\cdot$ norm with tuned $\lambda$; related (UML §7.7 remark), not identical; no claim that $\lambda$-tuning inherits Thm 4.4.
7. **MDL/Occam (§7.3):** optional [O]; the description language must be fixed before data (p. 91); prefix-freeness gives Kraft $\sum2^{-\lvert h\rvert}\le1$.
8. **Measurability:** each $\mathcal H_k$ satisfies (M); event of Lemma 4.3 is a countable intersection; selection rules measurable (assumed, as Định nghĩa 01.4).
9. **Bounded loss:** all formal results in $[0,1]$; polynomial regression is illustration only unless $\lvert Y\rvert\le B$ and clipping are added; squared-loss uniform bounds for polynomial classes are not available from Lectures 01–03 (pseudo-dimension is out of scope).
